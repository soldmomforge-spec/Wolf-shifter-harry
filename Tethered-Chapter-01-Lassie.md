# Tethered

## Chapter One: Lassie

---

### I. Bella

The smoke from Irina's body was still rising.

It came off the far side of the clearing in a thin, steady column, the colour of a bruise going old, and the wind off the mountains kept bending it toward the Cullen line and then thinking better of it. Bella could taste it at the back of her throat every time she breathed, which she didn't have to do and kept doing anyway. Sweet. Heavy. Incense in a church nobody had aired out in a hundred years.

Kate was still shaking. Bella could hear it without turning her head, the tiny dry rattle of Kate's teeth, the crackle of current under her skin that kept jumping into Garrett's palm where he held her forearm. Garrett hadn't let go since Caius had said the word *liar* and Felix and Demetri had moved. He'd taken three shocks that would have dropped Emmett and he was still holding on, jaw locked, eyes on the black cloaks.

Tanya had gone silent. That was worse.

The snow had come in the night, the way Alice had seen it, and it lay across the field in a skin about four inches deep, blue-white under a lid of cloud. It squeaked under nobody's feet. A hundred-odd people stood on it and none of them shifted their weight. On the far side, the Volturi held their formation like a chess problem: the ancients in the centre, their cloaks so dark they swallowed light; the guard fanned out on either side in charcoal and smoke-grey; Jane and Alec small and close at Aro's flank; Felix enormous behind them. Renata was pressed so close to Aro's shoulder she might have been sewn to it. Further back, two hooded women stood between a pair of bodyguards, and behind them, loose and uneasy, the Volturi's own witnesses waited. Forty of them, more or less. Bella had stopped counting when the number stopped mattering.

Alec's mist was still coming.

She could see it if she let herself look. It crawled across the snow toward them, low and patient, a grey fog that didn't stir the powder it moved over. It reached the edge of her shield and stopped there, pooling, feeling along the boundary the way water feels along a dam. Bella held. The shield sat out from her in every direction like a second skin she'd learned to stretch, around Edward, around Carlisle and Esme, around Rosalie with her lips drawn back off her teeth and Emmett crouched with his weight on the balls of his feet, around the Irish and the Egyptians and the Amazons, around Peter and Charlotte and the Romanians who had stood at the back all morning grinning like men watching a house they hated finally catch fire. Around the wolves. Around Jacob.

Around Renesmee.

Her daughter sat on Jacob's back with her fists wound into the russet fur at his shoulders and the backpack strapped tight between his shoulder blades, the one with the passports and the cash and the letter. Bella had put her there four minutes ago. She'd kissed her hair and said *I love you more than my life* and Renesmee had put a hand to her cheek and shown her Bella's own face, smiling, which had nearly ended her right there in the snow.

If it went wrong, Jacob would run. That was the deal. He'd run and he wouldn't stop.

Across the field, Aro had drifted back into the dark knot of his brothers. Caius was talking low and fast. Bella caught pieces through the wind: *unknown*, *the child's nature*, *we cannot allow*. Marcus stood a little apart from both of them, staring at nothing with the expression of a man watching rain through a window. Aro's hands were folded. Every so often he tipped his head toward Caius, listening, and every so often he looked over the snow at Renesmee with an expression Bella had seen on the faces of collectors at auctions.

Edward's hand tightened on hers.

"They're going to vote," he breathed, so low it barely made sound. "Caius has the votes he needs. Aro wants the shield and he wants Alice and he thinks there's a way to get both."

"And Renesmee?"

He didn't answer.

So that was that. Bella settled into her feet. She felt the shield the way she felt her own hands, every inch of its edge, and she pushed it out a little further, thickening it where the mist pooled. On her left, Zafrina had gone still in the particular way she went still before she put a picture in somebody's head. On her right, Benjamin had his hands loose at his sides and the ground under the snow had started, very faintly, to hum.

Then the air in the middle of the field cracked.

The sound was huge. It hit the clearing like a rifle shot and slapped back off the trees, and every single vampire in that field moved at once, a ripple of shoulders and knees and bared teeth on both sides, a hundred predators dropping half an inch lower at the same instant. Somewhere behind her a wolf barked. The snow at the centre of the field jumped as if something had landed on it from a height.

Something had. Someone.

A man stood in the open ground between the two lines, exactly halfway, where nobody had dared to stand all morning.

Bella's first thought was *human*. She heard his heart before she properly saw him. It was slow and heavy, a deep double knock, and it filled the silent field the way a single drum fills a cathedral. Her throat went tight on instinct. Three months of discipline, of holding her breath around Charlie, of hunting elk until her stomach was full and her mouth still ached, and her body tensed for the scent that would come with that heartbeat.

The wind turned. It brought him to her.

Pine. Turned earth after rain. Cold stone, the inside-of-a-cave smell of the rocks on First Beach. Under it something she couldn't name, something sharp and animal that made the hair on her arms lift.

Her throat didn't burn.

She stood there with her lungs full of him, waiting for the fire, and it didn't come. He smelled the way the forest smelled, and nothing in her wanted to drink him.

She looked properly.

He was tall, six two or near it, broad through the shoulders and dense with it, the kind of heavy muscle that sat close to the bone. Dark hair, a mess, too long at the back. A brown leather jacket worn soft and pale at the elbows, open over a dark green flannel shirt and a grey T-shirt. Jeans. Scuffed boots. No gloves. No hat. No coat worth the name in a Washington January. Around his boots, the snow was going to slush in a circle about a foot wide, and as she watched, a thin feather of steam lifted off his shoulders into the cold air and blew away.

He had green eyes. Bright green. Bella could see them from across forty yards of snow and they were looking, without any hurry at all, from one side of the field to the other.

He took his hands out of his jacket pockets.

"Well," he said. "This is cosy."

English. The accent was English, rough around the vowels, London or near it. His voice carried without his raising it.

Nobody answered him. Across the field, Aro had turned around. Caius had stopped mid-sentence. Jane's small pale face had gone very smooth, and Bella knew that look; she'd seen it pointed at Edward in Volterra a lifetime ago.

The man looked down at the slush around his boots, then up at the sky, then at the column of purple smoke still lifting off the remains of Irina. Something moved at the corner of his mouth. He sniffed once, the way you'd sniff milk that might have turned.

"Right," he said. "Evening, all. Afternoon. Whatever it is in this soggy bloody corner of the world." He reached into the inside of his jacket, and fifty vampires tensed to spring, and he came out with a little folded leather wallet and flipped it open toward the Volturi with two fingers, the way a cop on television flips open a badge. Something inside it caught the flat grey light and shimmered, silver going to gold going to silver. "Harry Potter. Department of Mysteries. Unspeakable, which technically means I'm not meant to tell you that, so if you could all forget it, cheers. I'm here on behalf of the International Confederation of Wizards."

He turned on his heel, slow, and showed the wallet to the Cullen line too. Bella saw it from forty yards: a crest she didn't recognise, a seal, a photograph of the man that moved. The photograph blinked at her.

"By order of the Confederation," he said, "this gathering is dispersed. The lot of you. Cloaks, fangs, fur. Everybody goes home." He snapped the wallet shut and put it away. "I'll wait."

The silence after that was so complete Bella heard snow settle off a branch two hundred yards into the trees.

"Edward," she said, without moving her lips.

"I can't hear him." Edward's voice had gone strange. He sounded younger. "I can't hear anything. Bella, it's like you. It's like you were, before. Except—" He stopped. His hand had gone stiff in hers. "Your silence was soft. His has edges."

"Is he one of us?"

"He's got a heartbeat."

"I know he's got a heartbeat, Edward."

"Then I don't know." He was staring at the man. "Everybody else, I can hear. Aro's fascinated. Caius thinks he's a madman. Jane wants to try her gift on him so badly she's shaking. Half their witnesses want to leave. Carlisle's—" He swallowed. "Carlisle's heard the word *wizard* before. A long time ago. In London."

Bella glanced at Carlisle. His face had gone the particular kind of blank it went when he was very frightened and working hard not to show it.

On her right, Garrett let out a long, slow breath through his nose. "Well," he murmured, to nobody in particular, still holding Kate's arm. "He's got a sense of occasion."

Kate had stopped shaking. She was staring at the stranger with her lips parted.

Behind Bella, the wolves were making a noise she couldn't place, a low unsettled whine passing from one throat to the next. She could hear Jacob breathing hard, short huffs through his nose, and she could feel the faint tremor in the snow where his paws were planted. Renesmee hadn't made a sound. Bella didn't dare turn around to look at her.

Across the field, Aro took a single step out from the dark knot of his brothers. Renata stepped with him like a shadow. He was smiling. Bella had learned to hate that smile in the space of a single morning, the papery delight of it, the way it creased his thin skin.

"How extraordinary," Aro said. His voice was light and pleasant and carried perfectly. "Forgive me, young man. You have rather taken us by surprise, and I confess that is not a thing that happens to me often. The International Confederation." He rolled the words around in his mouth as if tasting them. "Of *wizards*. How very quaint. I had understood that your people kept to yourselves. Your little parliament, your little laws."

"Yeah, we do," the man said. "Mostly. Then somebody upstairs noticed a hundred-odd vampires booking flights into Seattle in the same fortnight and a pack of very large dogs going walkabout on the Olympic Peninsula, and they got twitchy. So here I am. Missing my tea." He tipped his head toward Aro. "You'll be in charge, then. Of this lot."

"I am Aro."

"Course you are." The man looked past him, at Caius, at Marcus, at Jane and Alec and Felix and the rest of the dark cloaks spread out across the snow, and his expression didn't change at all. "Well, Aro. You heard me. Pack it in. Go back to Italy. Take the goth choir with you."

Caius's lip lifted off his teeth.

Bella felt it then, the change in the field, the thing that had been building all morning shifting its weight. Every eye that had been pointed at Renesmee was pointed at the stranger now. Every one. For the first time since the Volturi had come out of the trees, nobody was looking at her daughter.

She didn't know whether to be grateful or terrified.

Aro laughed, a soft, delighted sound. "You are very bold."

"I'm very knackered," the man said. "Long flight. Well. Not a flight." He rolled his shoulders under the leather jacket, a loose, heavy movement, and Bella saw the muscle shift under it. "I'll say it once more, nice and slow, because I'm a reasonable bloke. This gathering is dispersed under Confederation authority. The Statute of Secrecy applies to everyone standing in this field whether you've heard of it or not, and you lot are about thirty seconds off having a scrap that'd be visible from bloody orbit. That's not happening. Not on my watch, not fourteen miles from a town full of people doing their Saturday shopping." He held up one hand, fingers spread. "So. Everyone. Home."

Then he turned his head and looked at the Cullen line.

His gaze moved across them without stopping. Across Carlisle, across Esme. Across Rosalie, who hissed at him. Across Bella herself, and for half a heartbeat she felt the weight of those green eyes like a hand on her chest, though there was nothing in them at all but attention. Across Edward.

Then past them. To the wolves.

### II. Leah

She'd decided an hour ago that if she was going to die today, she was going to take the big one with her.

Felix. The one built like a vending machine, standing behind the little blond witch-twins with his hood back and his red eyes on Jacob's throat. Leah had picked him out the way you pick a spot on a wall to stare at when someone's yelling at you. She'd worked out the angle. Go low under his arms, take the knee, then the throat when he went down. She'd lose a leg doing it. Maybe her life. Fine. It'd be the most useful thing she'd done since she phased.

*Leah.* Seth, in her head, small and scared. *Stop planning that.*

*Stay out of my planning, kid.*

*Everyone can hear it.*

*Then everyone can stop listening.*

Jacob said nothing. He hadn't said much all morning. Nessie was on his back and his whole mind was a single bright wire pulled tight between her and the trees, the run he'd take if Bella gave the word. Leah had stopped trying not to hear that. Jacob's head had been all Nessie for four months, Nessie's curls, Nessie's laugh, Nessie's little hot hands, and if Leah had to swim through one more second of it she'd bite her own tail off. But today she let it run in the background, because today it meant something. Today it was the plan.

Then the guy appeared out of nowhere with a bang like a twelve-gauge, and her whole body tried to go in four directions at once.

*WHAT—*

*Where did he come from—*

*Is that a vampire? That's not—*

*He's got a heartbeat, Quil, listen to it—*

*He smells like—*

All five of them at once, a pile-up in the pack mind, and for a second Leah couldn't separate her own thoughts from theirs. Then they sorted themselves out and she was just Leah again, staring at a guy in a leather jacket standing in the middle of a hundred vampires with his hands in his pockets like he was waiting for a bus.

He talked. English, and cocky with it. He flashed a badge. Wizards. *Wizards.* Embry let out a mental noise like a laugh with the bottom kicked out of it. *Sure. Why not. Vampires, us, the creepy little mind-reader, the girl who can make you see things. Why not wizards.*

*Shut up,* Jacob said. *Listen.*

The guy told the Volturi to go home. The guy called them a goth choir. Seth made a strangled noise in the pack mind, half horror, half delight, and Leah felt her own lip twitch under her muzzle in spite of herself, and then hated that she'd felt it. Across the field, Caius looked like he'd swallowed a wasp.

Then the guy turned around and looked at the Cullens. One by one. Like he was counting them.

Then he looked at the wolves.

Leah looked back, because she wasn't about to look away from some stranger, wizard or not, because she'd never in her life been the first one to look away from anybody.

Green.

His eyes were green.

And the ground fell out from under her.

She'd heard it described. God, had she heard it described. She'd heard it from inside Sam's head, the morning he saw Emily, the morning he came back to Leah's house and couldn't look at her. She'd heard it from Jared, from Paul, from Quil the day he saw Claire, and from Jacob, four months of Jacob, every second of the moment it happened replayed in his memory like a song he couldn't stop humming. She knew what it was supposed to feel like. Everybody said gravity. Everybody said the whole world shifted and suddenly she was the only thing holding you to the earth. Everybody made it sound like a gift.

Nobody had told her it would feel like being gutted.

Every single thing that held her in place came loose at once. It went in a rush, all of it, a hundred cables she'd never known were there snapping in the same second: the rage she'd lived inside for two years, the ache that came every time Sam's mind brushed hers, her father's chair at the kitchen table that nobody sat in, her mother's face at the funeral, the long run of days where the only reason she got out of bed was that being angry was better than being nothing. The pack. The reservation. Her own name. They all went slack, every one of them, and she felt herself start to fall.

And then something caught her.

One line. One. It came out of the dead centre of her chest and ran in a straight, hard, humming line across sixty yards of snow, and it ended at a man in a leather jacket with green eyes and slush around his boots.

It held. God help her, it held, and she was hanging off it over nothing.

Leah's legs locked. Her claws went into the frozen ground under the snow. The sound that came out of her was nothing she'd ever made before, high and cracked and awful, a yelp like a kicked pup's, and she heard it go out across the clearing and hated it more than she'd ever hated anything.

The pack mind went white.

*Leah?*

*Leah, what—*

*Oh,* Seth said. Very quietly. *Oh no. Oh, Leah.*

*No.* She was thinking it at them so hard it hurt. *No. No no no no NO—*

*Did she just—* That was Quil.

*She did.* Embry, flat. *Look at her. She did.*

Jacob's mind swung toward hers like a searchlight. For one second all that Nessie-brightness turned and fell on Leah and she felt him *see* it, saw him recognise it, saw him reach for the memory of his own moment and lay it next to hers and watch them match. She felt his shock. Worse, she felt his pity.

*Leah,* Jacob said. *Leah, hey. Hey. Breathe.*

*Get out of my head.*

*I can't. You know I can't. Leah, it's okay—*

*It is NOT okay. Don't you dare. Don't you dare tell me it's okay, Black, you of all—* She couldn't finish. The line in her chest pulled and she felt her whole body lean with it, forward, toward the middle of the field, toward him, and she dug her claws in harder and held herself back by main force. *I didn't want this. I never wanted this. I said I'd never, I said if it ever happened to me I'd—*

*We know,* Seth said. *We know, Lee. We heard you say it.*

She had said it. More than once. Phased, in the pack mind, at three in the morning when the only one awake was Seth and she didn't care who heard. *If it ever happens to me I'll kill myself before I let it make me into one of them. Some smiling idiot following a stranger around. I'll walk into the sea.* She'd meant it. She'd believed every word.

The line hummed. She didn't walk anywhere.

And under the horror, under the screaming in her own head, the part of her that was wolf did something she would never forgive it for. It went quiet. It put its ears forward. It looked at the man across the snow and it was *calm*, for the first time in two years, it was calm.

She heard his heart. Across sixty yards, through the wind, through the silence of a hundred vampires, she heard it like it was beating against her own ribs. Slow. Heavy. A big heart in a big chest.

The wind shifted, and his scent reached her.

Pine. Wet earth. Stone, the cold rocks at the bottom of a creek.

And under it.

Leah's nose wrinkled hard on its own. She pulled the air in again, deeper, sorting it the way she'd sort a trail. Under the forest smell there was something else, something thick and dark and rank. Fur. Musk. Old blood and frost and something with teeth.

*Jake,* she said. Her voice in the pack mind came out small. *Jake, smell him.*

*He smells like pine.*

*Under it.* She was shaking. *Smell under it.*

Jacob dragged in a breath through his nose. She felt him do it, felt the scent come into his head through his own senses and sit next to hers. Felt him find it. Felt him go still.

Embry got there half a second later. *That's,* he said, and stopped. *That's wolf.*

*That's not us,* Quil said. *That's not any of us. That's not Sam's either—*

*He's not Quileute,* Seth said. *He's English. He's—how can he be—*

Nobody had an answer.

Across the field, the man had stopped moving. His head had come back around, slowly, until his eyes were on her. On *her*. There was a small crease between his brows. One hand came up out of his jacket pocket and rose a couple of inches, toward his own chest, toward the middle of his sternum, right where the line went in. It stopped halfway. Hung there a second. His eyes narrowed a fraction, the way a man's eyes narrow when he's trying to hear something a long way off.

Then he put his hand back in his pocket and turned his head back to the Volturi as if nothing had happened.

Leah made the yelping sound again. She couldn't stop it.

*Okay,* Jacob said, in the tone he used when he was trying to be Alpha and didn't know how. *Okay. Everybody hold. Nobody move. Leah—Leah, can you hold?*

*I'm holding.* She was. Barely. Every muscle in her body was singing with the need to cross that snow and stand next to him, put herself between him and the black cloaks, between him and anything. *I'm holding, I'm holding, I hate this, I hate you, I hate him—*

*You don't,* Seth said. Gently. *You can't. That's sort of the whole thing.*

*Seth, I swear to God—*

*His name's Harry,* Seth said.

The whole pack went quiet.

Leah felt it land in all of them at once. *Harry*. Their dad's name. Harry Clearwater, who'd had a heart attack in the kitchen the week Leah first phased, who'd died with his hand on the counter while she stood on the porch with her skin splitting open. She felt Seth's grief flicker, old and young at the same time. She felt Jacob flinch. She felt Quil and Embry try to look away from it, which was impossible in here, which was always impossible.

*Of course it is,* Leah said. *Of course it is. Of course it's fucking Harry.*

Nobody told her to watch her language.

On the far left of the clearing, Sam's pack had started to move. She could see them out of the corner of her eye, the big black shape of Sam at the front, Paul's silver beside him, Jared, Collin and Brady at the edge, all of them with their heads turned toward Jacob's side of the line. They couldn't hear any of this. Different pack, different mind. But they could see. They'd heard the noise she'd made. They could see the way Jacob's pack had broken formation, Seth crowding in against her shoulder, Quil and Embry turned half around to stare at her instead of at the Volturi.

They could see the way she was standing. Leaning forward against nothing. Ears pricked. Eyes locked on the stranger.

Sam's head turned. Sam looked at her.

She felt the moment he understood. She didn't need to be in his head for that. She'd been in his head for two years, she knew every inch of him, she knew what his ears did when something hit him in the gut. They went flat. His whole big black body went still, and then, very slowly, he lowered his head.

Something in her that she'd thought was dead and buried turned over and howled. *Don't you look at me like that. Don't you DARE feel sorry for me, Uley, don't you dare be relieved—*

*Leah.* Jacob's voice. *Leah, eyes front. Something's happening.*

She dragged her gaze back to the middle of the field, which was easy, because the middle of the field was where he was.

Caius had stepped forward. He'd come out from behind Aro's shoulder with his white hair pushed back and his face pulled tight with contempt, and his voice when he spoke had a sound in it like a blade on a whetstone.

"You have no authority here, witch."

The man in the leather jacket tilted his head. "Wizard, mate."

"I care nothing for the distinction."

"Yeah, I can see that. Witches are the ones with the better hats." He looked Caius up and down, slowly, from his white hair to the hem of his black cloak where it brushed the snow. "Whoever does your wardrobe, though, I'd have a word. You look like you've come to a funeral. Your own, possibly."

Somebody on the Cullen side made a choked noise. Emmett, Leah thought. Trying not to laugh.

Caius's face went perfectly white, even for a vampire.

*Is he insane?* Embry's thought was hushed. *Is he actually insane?*

*Doesn't matter.* Jacob, grim. *If they go for him, we hold. We don't break formation for some stranger. Nessie's the priority. Everybody hear me? Nessie's the priority.*

The line in Leah's chest jerked so hard she nearly fell over.

*Not mine,* she said, before she could stop herself. *Not MY priority.*

She felt Jacob's head whip toward her. Felt the pack mind go sharp and cold.

*Leah—*

*I know.* She was panting. *I know, I know, I'm holding, I'm not going anywhere, I'm just telling you, if they go for him I don't know what I'll do, Jacob, I don't KNOW—*

Across the snow, Aro lifted one long white hand. The gesture was small and elegant and Caius stopped moving the instant he saw it. Aro was smiling again, that thin, papery smile, and he stepped out past his brother into the open snow, with Renata gliding beside him like his shadow.

"Peace, brother," Aro said. "Peace. Our guest has come a long way, it seems, and he has come alone, which is either very brave or very foolish, and I find I am curious which." He opened his hand toward the man in the leather jacket, palm up, fingers spread. "Perhaps," he said, "you would allow me the courtesy of your hand?"

Leah's whole body went rigid.

*Don't,* she thought, at him, at the man, at Harry, before she could stop it. *Don't you dare let him touch you.*

Nobody heard that except the pack. He certainly didn't.

But he didn't take his hands out of his pockets either.

### III. Aro

He had touched a wizard once.

It had been in Florence, in the spring of 1487, in a narrow street behind the wool market where a man in a dark green robe had come out of a doorway that had not been there a moment earlier. Aro had been hungry and curious in roughly equal measure, as he so often was, and he had taken the man's wrist in the shadow of an archway and drunk the whole of his life through his skin before he drank the rest of him. A wooden stick in a sleeve. A hidden street full of crooked shops. Owls. A school in a cold country, a castle on a lake. Words in a dead tongue that made light come out of the stick's end. A fear of being *seen* that ran through every corner of the man's mind like damp through old plaster.

Aro had found it charming. A whole little world of mortals hiding behind their sticks and their Latin, terrified of the same cattle Aro fed on. He had told Caius about it over a pleasant evening, and Caius had sneered, and Marcus had said nothing at all, and they had agreed, without ever needing to say so, that the witch-folk were beneath notice. They kept their secrets. The Volturi kept theirs. In five hundred years, neither had troubled the other.

And now here stood one of them in the snow, with no stick in his hand, and no robe, and a heartbeat that filled the field like a drum.

Aro kept his palm open and his smile in place. He had held that smile through the fall of Rome.

"Your hand," he said again, gently. "A simple courtesy, between those who keep the peace."

The young man looked at Aro's open palm. Then he looked at Aro's face. One corner of his mouth went up.

"No, ta," he said. "I know what you do with that."

Aro's smile didn't move. Something behind it did.

"You read people," the young man went on, conversationally, as if he were remarking on the weather. "Skin to skin. Every thought they've ever had, all at once. Bit rude, if you ask me. No consent forms, nothing." He rocked back on his heels, hands still deep in his jacket pockets. "Confederation's got a file on you, Aro. Thick one. Your lot have been on the watch list since about the Borgias. You've got a girl who sets people's nerves on fire and a lad who switches them off, a tracker who can find anyone anywhere, a woman who ties people to you so tight they forget they ever had a choice. You've got a big lad who pulls heads off." He nodded past Aro's shoulder at Felix without looking at him. "Hiya, big lad. And you've got him." His eyes went to Marcus. "Him, I'm a bit fuzzy on. File says he sees relationships. Can't see that being much use at parties."

A file.

For the first time in a very long while, Aro felt something he had nearly forgotten the taste of. It sat cold and small in the pit of him, under the smile, under the curiosity. *They have been watching us. All this time. And we never knew.*

He let none of it reach his face. "You are remarkably well informed."

"I'm remarkably bored," said the young man. "And my feet are wet." He glanced down at the slush around his boots. "Well. They're not. But they would be, if I were a normal bloke, which, I'll be honest with you, is a ship that sailed a while back. Now. Are you lot dispersing, or what?"

Behind Aro, Caius hissed. "Brother. Enough of this."

"Patience."

"He insults us to our faces in front of witnesses. In front of *them*." Caius did not need to point at the Cullens. "Every moment we permit this, we are diminished."

That was true, and Aro knew it. He could feel the field watching. The Cullens and their ragged collection of friends, staring. The wolves, those great stinking beasts, in disarray on the left flank for reasons he could not see. And behind him, worst of all, his own witnesses: forty-some vampires he had brought across an ocean precisely so that they might watch the Volturi deliver justice and carry the tale of it home. Their silence had a texture to it. He could hear their feet shifting in the snow. He could hear somebody at the back whispering *wizard* to somebody else, and the tone of it.

Diminished. Yes.

And yet.

He breathed in, quietly, tasting the air. The young man's scent was strange. Forest, mostly. Earth and pine and cold stone, a scent with nothing in it to wet the throat. Aro had smelled a thousand humans in a thousand cities and he would have known this man for something other than human with his eyes closed. And under the forest, so faint he had to go looking for it, there was something else. Something rank and animal and old, a breath of the same stink that came off the shapeshifters on his left.

*What are you?*

A wizard who smelled of the dogs. Who stood in a Washington snowfield without a coat while the snow fled from his feet. Who knew about Aro's gift and declined, very politely, to be touched.

Aro felt the old hunger move in him, the one that had nothing to do with blood. The hunger that had made him collect Jane and Alec when they were children tied to a stake. That had made him want Edward, and Alice, and this morning the shield-girl Bella with a want so sharp it was very nearly pain. He looked at the young man in the snow and he wanted him, the way he wanted every rare and wonderful thing the world had hidden from him.

Marcus's hand touched his.

It was the lightest of touches, the back of a dry finger against Aro's knuckles, and Aro turned his palm over without thinking and closed it around his brother's hand. It was an old habit between them. Marcus rarely spoke. He did not need to.

The thoughts came, slow and grey and weary, as Marcus's thoughts had been ever since Didyme. *The bonds in this field.* The Cullens, bright and tangled and ferociously strong; Aro had seen those already. The wolves, a dull mass of loyalty. The child and the great russet wolf, a bond so bright and strange that Marcus had marked it this morning and never mentioned it.

And now. New. Minutes old.

The young man in the snow had a bond running out of him, and Marcus was looking at it.

It went from his chest across the field to the left flank, to one of the wolves. The small grey one, the one making that dreadful noise. In Marcus's sight it was a line of light. Aro saw it through his brother's memory. It was very bright, and very taut, and at the wolf's end of it there was nothing unusual: the familiar grip of the shapeshifters' strange devotion, the same shape as the russet wolf's bond to the child.

At the young man's end, it went in.

That was all Marcus could say about it. It went in, and in, and further in, deeper than a bond had any business going into anyone, down past anything Marcus had a name for, and at the bottom of it, Marcus could see something looking back.

Marcus's grey thoughts held one more thing, small and almost frightened, and that was the most extraordinary part of all, because Marcus had not been frightened of anything since he buried his wife.

*Brother. Do not touch this one.*

Aro let go of his hand.

He found he was smiling more widely than before.

"Jane," Caius said.

Aro did not stop him. He wanted to see.

Jane stepped forward half a pace. She did not need to. She liked to. She tipped her small blond head and looked at the young man with her wide red eyes and her lips parted very slightly, and Aro felt the tiny familiar shiver of anticipation he always felt when Jane went to work. The beautiful sound was coming. The scream. Even the bravest of them screamed.

The young man looked back at her.

Nothing happened.

Jane's brow creased. Her lips pressed together. Aro watched her lean into it, watched the effort move through her slight body, watched her small hands curl at her sides. For a girl who had brought kings to their knees by looking at them, it was a great deal of visible effort.

The young man blinked at her, slowly.

"Was that meant to tickle, sweetheart?" he asked. "Only you've got a face on you like you're trying to pass a kidney stone."

Jane made a sound. It was small and high and furious. Aro had not heard her make it since Bella.

"Huh," said the young man, peering at her. "Pain thing. Right. File said. Bit of a headache, actually, now you mention it. Like a tension one." He rolled his neck until it popped. "There. Gone."

On the Cullen side, someone laughed out loud. It was the large one, Emmett, and he slapped a hand over his mouth too late.

Alec's mist had already been in the snow for an hour. Aro had watched it crawl against Bella's shield like a dog against a locked door. Now, without a word from anyone, it changed direction. Alec had always been the quieter twin, the one who did not need an audience. The grey fog peeled off the edge of the shield and rolled toward the middle of the field, low and soft and silent, and reached the young man's boots, and touched the circle of slush around them.

And steamed.

It hissed away against him like water dropped on a hot iron. Aro watched the edge of the mist curl back, boil, thin out to nothing. The young man glanced down at it with mild interest, the way you'd glance at a puddle you'd stepped in.

"Oi," he said, not looking up. "Keep your fog to yourself, Damien."

Alec's face did not change. Alec's face never changed. But his mist stopped coming.

Two. Two in one day.

Aro felt dizzy with it. The girl, the shield, and now this man who simply *declined* to be affected, who brushed off the twins as if they were gnats; who knew things about the Volturi that the Volturi had never told a living soul; who could cross half a world without an aeroplane and arrive in the middle of a battlefield like a thunderclap. Whatever he was, wherever he came from, he was the rarest thing Aro had seen in five centuries, and he was standing alone in the snow, sixty feet away, surrounded.

*Do not touch this one,* Marcus had said.

Marcus was tired. Marcus had been tired for a very long time.

"Remarkable," Aro said. His voice came out softer than he meant it to. "Truly. You must forgive my brothers their manners. They have not had to deal with a guest of your quality in a very long time." He spread his hands. "But you will understand, I think, that we cannot simply leave. We came here to see justice done. A matter of law, among our kind. These witnesses"—a graceful sweep of his arm toward the forty behind him—"have travelled very far. It would be unseemly to send them home without a resolution."

"Resolution's this," said the young man. "You go home. They go home. Everybody lives. Brilliant."

"And if we decline?"

The young man took his hands out of his pockets.

He did it slowly, so that everyone could see. His hands were large and pale and empty. No stick. Nothing in them at all. He flexed the fingers of his right hand once, as if they were cold, which they plainly were not.

"Then I'll ask you one last time," he said. "Nicely. Because I'm told I'm meant to. This gathering is dispersed under the authority of the International Confederation of Wizards. Your lot. All of you in the cloaks." His green eyes went along the Volturi line from end to end, Jane, Alec, Felix, Demetri, Renata at Aro's shoulder, Chelsea and Afton, Santiago, Heidi, the wives in their hoods at the back with Corin, Caius, Marcus, Aro. They lingered on no one. They missed no one. "Disperse. Last time."

Aro looked at him for a long moment.

Then he looked at the snowfield, at the hundred watching faces, at the Cullens and their stolen shield and their impossible child. At his witnesses. At the wolves. He thought of the story that would be told tonight in a hundred hunting grounds across the world. *The Volturi came to Forks with every soldier they had, and a witch with no stick told them to go home, and they went.*

He thought of Caius. He thought of three thousand years.

He sighed.

"Felix," he said. "Demetri. Bring him to me. Gently." He smiled. "I want him whole."

Felix moved first. He always did. The ground shook under him as he came off the line, and Demetri flowed out at his side, lean and fast and silent, angling wide to come at the young man's back. Behind them the guard shifted, eager, every one of them leaning forward now that the order was given.

The young man watched them come. His expression didn't change.

"Yeah," he said, almost to himself. "Thought you'd say that."

He lifted his right hand to shoulder height, loose, and snapped his fingers.

The sound was small. A dry click, flesh on flesh, nothing more.

Aro felt heat.

It began in the centre of him, in the place where his heart had stopped three thousand years ago, and for one infinitely long instant he thought, with genuine astonishment, *I am warm.* Then the warmth became the sun. A forge door opening inside his chest. It came up through him from the inside out, through every vein and every channel the venom had carved, and his skin lit up gold from beneath like a paper lantern.

He saw Felix go, mid-stride, a man-shaped tower of white fire. He saw Demetri. He saw Caius beside him open his mouth to scream and saw the scream catch fire in his throat before it could leave. Jane, small and bright as a struck match. Alec. Renata's hand still on his sleeve, blazing. Marcus, who did not move at all, who closed his eyes as the light took him with something on his face that might almost have been relief.

At the very back of the line, two hooded figures bloomed gold.

*Sulpicia*, Aro thought.

Then there was no more Aro to think it.

### IV. Edward

The thoughts stopped.

That was what Edward would remember, afterwards, long after he'd forgotten the colour of the light. A hundred and sixty minds had been pouring into his head since dawn, a roar like a stadium, and a third of that roar came from the far side of the field. Aro's thoughts, silky and acquisitive, turning the stranger over like a jewel. Caius's, all spite. Jane's furious little shriek of failure. Felix's dull eagerness as he came off the line. Demetri, calculating angles. Renata, focused and afraid. Chelsea, tugging at threads. The wives, bored and cold. Marcus, grey as ash.

Then the dry click of a finger snap, and for one second the whole far side of the field went white.

Then it went silent.

All of it. Thirty-odd minds that had been broadcasting into Edward's skull since sunrise, gone at once, like someone had walked through a house switching off every radio in every room. He staggered. He actually staggered, and Bella's hand clamped onto his arm, and he stood there in the snow with half the noise in his head simply missing and the silence where it had been ringing in his ears like the aftermath of a gunshot.

Then the ash came down.

It fell slow. Grey and fine, like the ash that falls downwind of a forest fire, sifting out of the air over the place where the Volturi had been standing. A great heavy cloud of purple smoke boiled up off the field in a single roiling column and rolled over on itself and climbed, and the smell hit the Cullen line half a second later, the sweet, cloying, church-incense stink of burning vampire, so thick Edward could taste it on his tongue. Under the smoke, where every member of the Volturi had stood, there was a small grey heap on a patch of bare wet grass. Each heap had its own circle, a yard wide, where the snow had flashed straight to steam. Felix's was long and smeared, because he'd been running. Jane's was very small.

The two at the back, where the wives had stood, lay side by side.

There were no cloaks. No bones. No scraps of anything. Just ash, and wet grass, and the smoke going up into the white sky.

One second. Edward had heard the snap and he'd heard the silence and between them there had been exactly one second, and in that second the most powerful family of vampires on earth had ceased to exist.

The minds that were left started screaming.

*—what—what did he—*

*—gone they're GONE—*

*—Aro—*

*—run, run, RUN—*

The Volturi's witnesses broke first. Forty-some vampires who had crossed an ocean to watch a child die, standing in a loose crowd behind a row of ash heaps that had been their masters, and their thoughts went up in a single shrieking wave of terror and then they *ran*. They scattered. They went in every direction at once, into the trees, up the slopes, south along the ridge, a burst of blurred shapes so fast that the snow kicked up behind them in long sprays and hung in the air after they'd gone. Edward heard their minds stream away from him through the forest, shrinking, thinning, every one of them thinking the same thing in a dozen languages. *Away. Away. Get away from him.*

In the middle of the field, the stranger watched them go. He didn't move to stop them.

"Yeah," he called after them, mildly. "Off you go. Mind the trees."

Then it was quiet, apart from the minds on Edward's side of the field, which were very far from quiet.

*Holy—* Emmett, crouched, frozen. *Holy shit. Holy shit. Rose, did you see—*

*Our Father, who art in heaven—* That was Carlisle, and Edward hadn't heard Carlisle pray in his head like that, in the old words, in the old voice, in eighty years. *—hallowed be thy name—*

*The children.* Esme. *Where are my children. Edward, Bella, Rosalie, Emmett, Renesmee, Jasper and Alice aren't here, thank God, thank God they aren't here—*

*He did that with a snap.* Benjamin, wide-eyed. *With a snap of his fingers. Amun, Amun, are you seeing—*

*I see.* Amun, sour with fear. *I see we should not be here.*

Kate's mind was a single long note. *Irina.* The ones who'd ordered it, the ones who'd held her down, the ones who'd lit the fire. Twenty minutes. Twenty minutes ago her sister had been alive and screaming and now the creatures who had done it were ash in the snow, and Kate didn't know what she felt, and the not-knowing was tearing her open. Beside her, Tanya had sunk to her knees in the slush and was staring at the place where Caius had stood with her face completely empty.

Garrett, still holding Kate's arm, thought: *Well. There goes the revolution.* And then, a moment later, very carefully, in a mind that had followed every rebel cause on two continents for three hundred years: *Who in God's name is he.*

Zafrina had pulled her pictures back. Senna had moved to stand in front of her. The Irish had drawn together, Siobhan's hand on Maggie's shoulder, Liam a step in front of both of them. Peter had taken Charlotte's hand and was thinking about the shortest route to the trees.

And at the back of the line, the Romanians.

Edward had been hearing Stefan and Vladimir all morning. He'd been hearing them for weeks, ever since they'd turned up at the house with their papery skin and their dark red eyes and their delighted, hungry malice. They'd come for one reason and they'd never pretended otherwise. They wanted to see the Volturi fall. They wanted to be standing there when it happened. Fifteen hundred years they'd waited, ever since Aro's guard had come over the mountains and burned their castles and their covens and their kingdom, and they'd dreamed of this, of the day it all came down, of their own hands doing the tearing.

Edward heard what was happening in their heads now and his stomach turned over.

*No.* Vladimir. *No. NO. That was ours.*

*Brother—*

*That was OURS, Stefan. Fifteen centuries. Fifteen centuries we waited. We were going to watch Caius beg. I was going to take Aro's hands myself—* Vladimir's thoughts were splintering, rage on rage, grief so old it had calcified into something else entirely. *And this, this mortal child, this nothing, he walks in from the air and he snaps his fingers and he takes it, he takes it from us, he TAKES—*

*Vladimir, be still.*

But Edward could hear what was underneath it, and it was worse than the rage. Vladimir was looking at the stranger and seeing the shape of the thing he hated. A power that came out of nowhere and burned everyone who refused to kneel. A new master. The old Volturi had ended, and here in the snow stood whatever came after.

*He'll be worse,* Vladimir thought. *They always are. Kill it now. Kill it while it's turned. It's looking at the Volturi's ash. It's not looking at us.*

Edward heard the decision a quarter of a second before the body moved, the way he always did. A quarter of a second was a long time for Edward. Long enough to open his mouth. Long enough to start to turn.

"Vladimir," he said. "*Don't*—"

Vladimir leapt.

He came off the back of the Cullen line like a thrown spear, straight up and over Siobhan's head, an arc that would bring him down out of the white sky onto the stranger's unprotected back with sixty feet of speed behind him. He was fast. He was very old and very fast and Edward, watching it happen, couldn't think of a single living vampire who could have turned in time to meet it.

The stranger didn't turn.

Vladimir caught fire in midair.

There was no snap this time. No gesture at all. The stranger stood exactly as he was, looking at the ash where Aro had been, and twenty feet above and behind him Vladimir simply went up, from the inside out, gold-white, a man-shaped flare against the sky. One second. If Vladimir screamed, Edward never heard it. Vladimir's mind went from *kill* to nothing so fast there was no space in between.

The ash came down in a long fan across the snow. Some of it fell on the stranger. A few grey flakes settled on the shoulder of his leather jacket.

He glanced at them. Brushed them off with the back of his hand, two short strokes, the way you'd brush off a bit of lint.

"Anyone else?" he asked.

Stefan screamed.

It was the first sound any vampire on the Cullen side had made out loud, and it tore across the field raw and enormous, and Stefan was moving before it finished. One step, two, his dark hair wild, his red eyes blazing, his whole body flung forward at the stranger like a dog going for a throat—

—and on the third step, he stopped.

Edward heard why. He heard it all at once, the whole terrible calculation flashing through Stefan's mind in less time than it took the step to land. *Vladimir is gone. The Volturi are gone. He did not look. He did not even look.* Fifteen hundred years at his brother's side. He stood there swaying in the snow with his hands curled into claws and his mouth open, and every line of him wanted to go forward, and he did not go forward. He stood there.

So did everyone else.

Edward felt the hesitation go through the Cullen line like a wave through tall grass. Emmett, who'd come up out of his crouch the instant Vladimir moved, whether to stop him or join him even Emmett didn't know, settled back on his heels very slowly. Rosalie's lips came down over her teeth. Kate's current guttered out. The faint hum Benjamin had been building in the ground under the snow died away to nothing. Every mind on Edward's side of the field had gone the same direction at the same time, and the direction was *still*.

*Don't move,* Carlisle was thinking. *Don't anybody move. Please. Please God, nobody move.*

Then Carlisle moved.

He did it the way he did everything, carefully, with his hands open and out at his sides, a step forward out of the line and onto the open snow. His face was composed. His mind was a wall of terror with a single steady thought held up in the middle of it like a lamp: *Talk to him. We have to talk to him. Make him see we aren't a threat.*

"Please," Carlisle said. His voice was even. "Sir. We mean you no harm. None of us. Vladimir acted alone, and I'm very sorry for it. My name is Carlisle Cullen. These are my family and our friends. We came here only because—"

"Hold that thought, Doc," said the stranger.

He hadn't turned around. He'd said it to the ash.

Then he did turn around, and Edward saw his face for the first time since the snap. It was the same face. That was the worst of it. It hadn't changed. The same sharp jaw, the same green eyes, the same faint, tired, dry expression of a man at the end of a long day. There was no triumph in it. There was no strain. He'd just killed more than thirty vampires in the space of two seconds and he looked like somebody who'd taken the bins out.

His eyes went over Carlisle. Over Stefan, still swaying. Over the rest of them.

And then, between one second and the next, they were on Renesmee.

Edward felt it before he understood it. He'd been hearing his daughter all morning, as he heard her every waking moment of every day, a bright little stream of colour and sound running under everything else. Pictures, mostly. Renesmee thought in pictures. She'd been thinking in frightened ones all morning: Aro's face, Aro's hands, Momma's face, Jacob's fur under her fingers, the backpack and the passports and the long run through the trees that she knew was coming because she'd heard her parents plan it when they thought she was asleep.

Now the pictures began to move on their own.

They turned over. They *flipped*. One after another, fast, much too fast, faster than Renesmee had ever thought in her life. The clearing this morning. The drive here in the dark. Last night, Momma singing to her. Last week, the snow coming down outside the cottage window. Christmas. Grandpa Charlie's face, his moustache, the smell of his coat. Hunting with Daddy in the dark, the deer's hot pulse. Irina on the ridge, staring, horrified. Jacob, Jacob, Jacob. Rosalie's arms. Her first steps. Her first word. The taste of blood from a cup. Further. Further back. Being small. Being new. Being—

Somebody else was turning the pages.

Edward couldn't hear him. That was the horror of it. He could hear *Renesmee* and he could hear what was happening to her, the memories flicking past one after another like a deck of cards being riffled by an expert's thumb, but the thing that was doing it had no voice at all. Where a second mind should have been, Edward found a pressure, thin and cold and very sharp, and Renesmee's thoughts parting around it the way water parts around the prow of a boat.

Beside him, Bella gasped.

It was a sound of pain. Edward had heard every sound Bella's body could make as a human and he'd learned most of the sounds it made now, and he'd never heard that one. She bent half over, one hand flying to her temple, and her shield, the great invisible bubble that had sat over all of them since morning like a held breath, *rippled*. Edward felt it go past him like a change in air pressure.

"He cut it," Bella said. Her voice was thin with shock. "Edward. He cut right through it. It was like a, a blade, like a razor, it went straight through—"

Edward was already moving.

His body made the decision before he did, the same way it would have done it if a car had come at her, if a nomad had stepped out of the trees. He went toward Renesmee and toward the stranger who was standing on the other side of the snow with his eyes locked on Edward's daughter, and his lips were peeling back off his teeth, and every thought in his head was gone except one—

"Don't, Romeo," the stranger said.

He didn't look away from Renesmee. He didn't raise his voice.

Edward stopped. He stopped dead, three strides out from the line, his whole body trembling with the need to go on. He couldn't have said why he stopped. Something in him that was older than thought had looked at the wide grey fan of Vladimir's ash on the snow and had simply refused to take another step.

"She's fine," the stranger said, still not looking at him. "She'll have a headache. Give me a sec."

Edward stood there with his fists clenched and his daughter's mind flipping and flipping and flipping in his head, faster and faster, all the way back, toward the dark and the warm and the sound of a heart beating through a wall of skin.

"He's inside her head," he said. His voice came out strange and flat, like somebody else's. "Bella. He's inside her head."

### V. Harry

The kid had been bugging him since he arrived.

He'd clocked her in the first two seconds, the way he clocked exits and weapons and whoever was standing closest to whoever looked most likely to do something stupid. A small girl, three, maybe four, bronze curls, red wool coat, sitting on the back of a russet wolf the size of a horse with her fists buried in its fur. In a field full of vampires. With a heartbeat.

That had been the first thing wrong with her. The heartbeat. It was too fast. He'd had a godson that age once, and he'd held him asleep on his chest more times than he could count, and he knew what a small child's heart sounded like. This one went like a bird's. Like a hummingbird's, a fine fast flutter that never slowed.

The second thing was her smell. Blood, and under the blood something cold and sweet and chemical that he'd been smelling on every pale face in this field since he landed. Venom. The kid smelled like a living child and like one of them at the same time, both scents knotted together so tight he couldn't find the seam.

The third thing was that thirty-odd vampires from Italy had flown halfway round the world with their whole army and their wives to kill her.

The Confederation file on that had been one line long. *Volturi mobilisation, full strength, cause believed to concern a child.* That was all anyone had got. He'd been briefed in a corridor in Geneva by a tired woman holding a cup of coffee, and she'd said *we think it's about a child, Harry*, and shrugged, and he'd said *a child,* and she'd said *that's the word our source used,* and he'd gone.

So. The child.

She was staring at him. Kids stared; it was one of the best things about them. She'd been staring at him since the moment he arrived, with huge chocolate-brown eyes that didn't belong in a vampire's face, and she was staring at him now, over the wolf's shoulder, with her lips parted and her face gone white under the curls.

Eye contact. Lovely. Made it easier.

He went in.

He didn't need the word anymore. He hadn't needed it in years. He'd spent seventeen years with a sliver of the darkest wizard in a century jammed into his skull like shrapnel, and when it finally came out, it left the door it had used behind it. After the war, he'd found he could walk through that door into other people the way Voldemort had walked into him. He'd spent a long time learning how. He'd spent a longer time learning how to do it better than the man who'd taught him by accident. He'd been taught Occlumency badly by a man who hated him; he'd taught himself Legilimency well, out of spite and necessity, in a basement in the Department of Mysteries, on volunteers who were paid extremely well and signed a great many forms.

He was good at it now. He didn't take any particular pleasure in that.

The first thing he hit was the shield.

He'd felt it when he landed. A big soft dome of something stretched over the whole Cullen side of the field, the wolves too, like cling film over a bowl. Whatever it was, it ran on will. Pure stubborn will, stretched thin and wide and held there, and it was very good. He could feel how good it was. Whoever was holding it had been holding it for hours, and it hadn't so much as flickered when the Volturi's little blond horror had been throwing herself at it all morning. It was strong where it needed to be strong and soft where it needed to give. Somebody had built it out of love and stubbornness and nothing else, and it was humming.

Nobody had built it with Legilimency in mind. It stopped it anyway. It stopped everything that went at the mind, by the feel of it, and the mind he wanted was on the other side.

He could have battered it. He could feel exactly how much force it would take to punch a hole straight through it, and he could feel what that would do to whoever was holding it. So he went looking. Ran his attention along the curve of it the way you'd run your thumb along a blade, feeling for the thinnest place. Found it, low on the left side, where the shield stretched furthest to cover the wolves and the kid on the wolf's back.

He put an edge on his will. Fine as a hair. Then he drew it.

The shield parted.

It parted the way silk parts under a razor, clean, a single straight line, and he slid through the gap and felt it close behind him almost at once. Almost. Going through a shield always left a mark. You couldn't cut a thing and have it be the way it was before. You could be as careful as you liked, and he had been careful, but there'd be a seam in it now, fine and pale, where his edge had gone, and whoever was holding it would have felt the cut like a paper cut across the inside of her skull.

Somewhere off to his left, a woman gasped.

Fine. Couldn't be helped.

The kid's own mind was on the other side, and it had a sort of defence of its own, a shimmer at the surface like the surface of a soap bubble. Natural. Untrained. He went through that the same way, one clean line, and felt it mark too. Felt the kid flinch from it, in here, in her own head, the way she'd flinch from a cold hand.

Then he was inside, and it was like falling into a painting.

Most minds were words and pictures and noise all tangled up together. Hers was almost all picture. Colour, light, smell, texture, all of it vivid and enormous and pin-sharp, like the world through a child's eyes always was, only more. Much more. He'd been in the minds of a few kids over the years, when he'd had to, when there'd been no other way, and none of them had been like this. This was like stepping into a cinema with the volume turned all the way up.

She felt him. Course she did.

And she did something he hadn't expected: she pushed back with *pictures*. She flung them at him, one after another, hard as she could, the way a kid throws toys at a stranger who's come into her bedroom. A wolf's teeth, huge and white and snapping, Jacob's teeth. A woman's face, furious, red-eyed, beautiful, Momma's face. A bronze-haired boy with a snarl on his mouth, Daddy. A door slamming shut. A door slamming shut. A door slamming shut, the image bright and loud and desperate, *GO AWAY GO AWAY GO AWAY*.

Interesting. She sent her pictures as well as thinking them. That was a gift, then. Like the blond girl's pain, like the tracker's tracking. Like the shield.

He stepped round the pictures and left them whole. He went past them the way you'd go past a kid who'd planted herself in a doorway, a hand on her shoulder, moving her aside, and he went straight down into the stacks.

He wasn't gentle about it. There wasn't time to be gentle and he'd stopped pretending, a long time ago, that there was a gentle way to do this. There wasn't. Gentle was slow, and slow was the kid sitting in a field full of panicking vampires while some bloke in her head took his time over her nursery memories. So he kept it precise. One line in, one line out, take what he needed and touch nothing else. A blade. A hammer left bruises everywhere. A blade only left a cut.

He started at the top and went backwards.

This morning: snow, dark, cold, Momma's arms. Aro. That one came up fast and ugly, dripping with fear, a papery white face bending close, a dry hand on her cheek and the feeling of being *read*, her whole little life pulled out through her skin. Harry felt a flicker of something mean go through him that the bastard was ash.

Irina on a ridge, weeks ago, staring down at her with her face going slack with horror. The same woman this morning, screaming, and then purple smoke.

Back.

A cottage in the woods, firelight, Momma singing. A big house full of glass, a blond man with a tape measure wrapping it round her head and writing numbers on a chart, *Grandpa Carlisle*, the chart full of lines that climbed and climbed. Clothes that didn't fit anymore. Clothes that didn't fit anymore. A whole wardrobe of outgrown clothes, folded in a box. The kid knew she was growing too fast. She didn't understand what it meant. The grown-ups did, though. She'd seen their faces when they looked at the chart.

Christmas. A tree, lights, a man with a moustache and a police jacket who smelled of coffee and gun oil and who held her very carefully on his knee as if she might break. *Grandpa Charlie.* Human, that one. Had a pulse like a human. Didn't know what she was. Thought she was something else, something they'd told him; she wasn't sure what.

Blood in a sippy cup. Blood in a mug. Hunting at night, Daddy's hand around hers, a deer's hot heartbeat under her teeth. Human food on a plate, eggs, horrible, pushed away.

A name. It came to him as a sound, the way kids know their names before they know how they're spelled. *Ruh-NEZ-may.* Renesmee. And a second one, shorter, softer, *Nessie*, and that one came wrapped in russet fur and a big laughing face and the smell of the sea.

Jacob.

Jacob was everywhere. Jacob was in every memory, at the edge of every room, at the side of every bed, at the bottom of every frightened moment like the floor under her feet. Jacob as a wolf, huge and hot. Jacob as a boy, tall and brown and laughing. Jacob holding her up to see the snow. Jacob asleep on the floor beside her cot.

Jacob, the very first time. She was tiny, brand new, and a face had come round a doorway, a boy's face, full of murder, and then he'd looked at her and the murder had just *gone*. His whole face had gone slack and wide, and he'd stared at her as if she were the sun coming up.

Harry looked at that one for longer than the others. It sat wrong with him. He couldn't have said why, yet.

He kept going.

Further back, and the pictures got smaller and simpler, newer, made by a brain that was days old and already sharper than it had any right to be. Being held up to a window. Blood from a bottle, warm. A golden-haired woman, *Aunt Rose*, rocking her. Daddy's face, close, his eyes wet. A woman lying very still on a table, *Momma*, and silence where her heartbeat should have been. A strange high whine coming out of Momma, on and on, and the smell of something burning from the inside.

And before that.

Red. Light, too much light, and red, and blood, and a tearing. A pale face with its teeth bared, *Daddy*, biting through something round her, a wall, a skin, to let her out. Hands lifting her. Cold air. Momma's face, white as paper, her eyes rolling. Momma's voice, a croak: *let me hold her*. Being held. Being so hungry. And the kid, minutes old, turning her head to her mother's chest and biting down, her new teeth going into her own mother's skin—

Before that, the pictures ran out. Dark, and warmth, and sound. A heartbeat. A huge slow heartbeat, all around her, that she knew the way she knew her own name. Voices through a wall. A man's voice: *Bella*. A woman's: *Edward*. The heartbeat getting slower. Weaker. Bones cracking somewhere above her, like ice going on a pond. The heartbeat getting weaker still. Her own small fierce hunger. And love, so much love coming through the walls of her world that it was like being held in a fist.

Harry stopped.

He hung there a second in the kid's oldest memory, in the dark, with that human heartbeat thumping all round him.

A human heartbeat. Her mother's. Her mother had a heartbeat, back then.

So her mum had been human. Her mum had been human and pregnant, and her dad had been, what, a vampire? A dead man, cold all the way through, no blood moving, no heart going. And somehow the two of them had made *this*. And then the kid had come out of her through her ribs, near enough, and her mother's heart had stopped, and something had burned, and now her mother was one of them.

That was who the kid was. Renesmee Cullen. Four months old, give or take, looked four years. Daughter of Bella, who'd been human, and Edward, who'd been dead for God knew how long. Half one thing and half another. Grew like a weed. Drank blood. Had a heartbeat. Showed you pictures if she touched you. Had a big red wolf who'd gone soppy the first time he saw her.

That was who.

He still had no idea *what*. The kid didn't know either. She knew she was different. She knew she was Momma's and Daddy's and Jacob's. She knew Momma had been human when she was in her tummy, because Aunt Rose had told her so, and Daddy had told her so, and she'd shown it to Aro this morning with her own hand. She didn't know how a dead man made a living baby. She didn't know what had happened to her mother's body to let it, or what the venom had done, or what she'd grow into, or when she'd stop. Nobody'd told her. Why would they? She was four months old.

Her mum would know.

He came out.

He came out the way he went in, one clean line, and he kept it as narrow as he could. It didn't make much difference. Going through a shield cost something on the way out as well, and it cost the kid. He felt the mark settle as he went, a thin pale line across the painting of her mind like a scratch on glass. She'd remember this. Probably always. A cold, sharp, silent thing coming into her head and going through her life like a knife through a drawer of photographs. She'd dream about it.

He was back in his own skull in the snow, with his own heartbeat in his ears and his own eyes looking at the kid across forty yards of field, and she was crying.

She'd gone white as the snow. Her eyes had filled and spilled over and her mouth was square with it, and she was clutching the wolf's fur so hard her small knuckles had gone grey. A single bright bead of blood had come out of one nostril. As he watched, it ran down over her lip.

Every vampire in that field smelled it at once. He saw the heads turn. Saw the blonde one at the back, *Aunt Rose*, go rigid. Heard the low, rising snarl come up out of the russet wolf underneath the kid, a sound like a lorry engine turning over.

"Yeah," Harry muttered. "Sorry, kid."

She'd have a headache. He hadn't lied about that.

He looked away from her. Across the snow, past the bronze-haired one who'd frozen three strides out with his fists shaking, to the woman beside him.

Brown hair, very long. A face like a painting. Red eyes, going amber at the edges. One hand pressed to her temple where his edge had gone through her shield, and her other hand reaching, already reaching, for her kid.

*Bella.*

The shield was pulling in. He watched it go, felt it go, the whole big soft dome of it sucking back in toward her like a tide going out, drawing in from the wolves, from the strangers at the back, from everyone, until it wrapped her and her husband and the kid on the wolf and nobody else. It got thick as it shrank. Thick as a wall. She'd felt him cut it, and she'd understood what that meant, and she'd decided to make sure he couldn't do it twice.

Clever girl.

He thought about the kid's oldest memory. The dark, and the heartbeat getting slower, and the sound of bones going.

He thought about the face in the doorway, the one that had come for murder and stayed for good.

He thought about a dead man and a living girl and whatever the hell they'd thought they were doing.

He didn't bother looking for the thin place.

### VI. Bella

The cut was still there.

She could feel it in her shield the way you feel a scratch on the roof of your mouth with your tongue, a fine straight line of cold across the inside of her skull where something sharp had gone through and out again. It had closed. Mostly. But there was a seam now, a thin pale ridge in the thing she'd built, and it hadn't been there a minute ago.

And her daughter was bleeding.

She smelled it before she saw it. One drop. One tiny bright drop of Renesmee's blood in the cold air, and every vampire left in the clearing turned their head toward it at once. Bella's own throat flared. She hated herself for it and turned the hate into something useful, which was rage.

She pulled the shield in.

She did it the way she'd practised with Kate, night after night in the snow behind the house, Kate's hands sparking against her skin until she learned how to fold the shield back in on herself and push it out again. This time she hauled. She dragged it in from everyone, the wolves, the Irish, Garrett and Kate, Carlisle and Esme, Rosalie, Emmett. She felt them go out of it one by one like lights going off down a corridor, and she was sorry, she was so sorry, but she didn't have enough. Not for everyone. Not against this. She brought it in until it held three people: Edward, standing frozen three strides out in the snow; Renesmee on Jacob's back; and her.

Then she made it thick.

She poured everything into it. Every scrap of will, every memory of every night she'd spent learning what it could do, all of Jane's useless fury bouncing off it this morning, all of Alec's mist crawling and crawling against it and never finding a way through. She made it a wall. She made it a vault door. She made it so thick she could barely see through it.

*Try it*, she thought, and lifted her head, and looked straight at him.

He was already looking at her.

Those green eyes. Across forty yards of snow and ash. His face was empty. He looked like a man glancing at a door and deciding not to knock.

He didn't knock.

It hit her like a train.

This time he used no blade, no neat straight line. Everything came at once, a single impossible weight slamming into the front of her shield from forty yards away, and the shield *caved*. She felt it buckle. She felt it fold inward like a car door in a crash, the whole beautiful stubborn thing she'd made crumpling and crumpling and then, with a sound in her head like tearing sheet metal, giving way.

Her knees hit the snow. She didn't remember falling.

He came in.

She'd always wondered what Edward heard. What it was like, to be inside a mind. She'd never thought about what it would be like to be the mind. It was like having the roof ripped off her skull. It was like a pair of hands reaching into her and tipping everything out onto the floor, every drawer, every box, every shelf. She could feel him going through her. *Rummaging*. Shoving things aside. Picking things up and dropping them. Moving fast, and hard, and with no care at all for what he broke.

Somewhere above her, Edward was roaring her name.

She couldn't answer. Her memories were going past her, out of order, faster than she could follow them. Charlie's kitchen. A biology classroom. A meadow. A mirrored room full of blood. An island. A bed. A cup. A table. Pain, so much pain. Fire.

And Jacob. Jacob's face. Jacob's face, looking at her daughter.

### VII. Harry

The woman's head was a mess.

The human part of it, anyway. Everything before a certain point was fogged, smeared, like old film left too long in a damp drawer: colours faded, edges gone soft, sound coming through as if from under water. It didn't matter. Legilimency went after whatever was *there*, remembered or not, and all of it was still there under the fog, every bit of it, if you were willing to dig.

He was willing.

Everything after that point was pin-sharp. Crystalline. Every memory cut glass. Easier.

He didn't bother being tidy about any of it. He'd spent his patience on the kid, and he'd seen enough in the kid's oldest memory to stop caring very much how this one felt about anything. He went straight through her life like a man looking for his car keys in a burning house.

A small town under rain. A cop father, *Charlie*, the same moustache from the kid's Christmas memory. A red truck that sounded like a tractor. A school. A biology classroom, and a boy with bronze hair two stools away, staring at her like he wanted to tear her throat out.

Harry slowed down on that one for half a second. Huh. Not the way he'd have started a romance, but he'd never been good at that stuff.

A van skidding on ice, and the boy stopping it with his bare hand.

A forest. A meadow. Sunlight, and the boy taking his shirt off in it, and his skin—

His skin *glittered*.

Harry, inside a stranger's head, in a snowfield in Washington, with a vampire army in ashes behind him, had a single clear thought, and the thought was: *Oh, you have got to be fucking kidding me.*

He kept moving.

A tracker, *James*, a smirking face, a ballet studio full of mirrors. A broken leg, a bite on the wrist, the bronze-haired boy with his mouth on the wound sucking the venom back out. A birthday party, a paper cut, a whole family of vampires lunging at her at once. The boy leaving. Months of nothing, grey, hollow, the girl sitting in a chair by a window while the seasons went past outside.

Then a different boy. A tall brown laughing boy with a garage full of bits of motorbike, and she was smiling again for the first time in months. *Jacob.* Running at night in the woods. Turning into a wolf in front of her, huge and russet and shaking. The same wolf, Harry noted. The same red wolf the kid was sitting on now.

Italy. The castle. Aro, his papery hand, the little blond girl staring. All of them now ash on the snow.

A newborn army, a wedding, a white dress. An island with white sand and a big white bed.

He went through the honeymoon fast. Very fast. A smashed headboard and feathers everywhere and bruises on her arms that she was oddly pleased about, and that was as much as he needed and a good deal more than he'd wanted.

Then, two weeks later, the girl on the bathroom floor, sick. A box of tampons in her hand that she hadn't needed to open. Her hand on her stomach. And under her hand, already, something moving.

*Oh*, Harry thought. *Oh, no.*

He went faster.

The boy's face, grey with horror. The boy's father, the blond doctor, *Carlisle*, saying *we don't know what it is* into a phone. The girl refusing. Refusing them all. A blonde woman, *Rosalie*, standing over her like a guard dog. And the thing inside her growing, growing by the day, by the hour, pushing her ribs out, eating her from the inside. Her face going hollow. Her skin going grey. Her bones breaking. He heard them break. Ribs. A pelvis, the crack of it like a branch going. And the girl lying on a sofa covered in a blanket, more bone than woman, sipping human blood out of a styrofoam cup through a bendy straw because nothing else would keep her and the thing inside her alive, while the red-wolf boy stood in the corner with his fists clenched and his eyes wet.

And then the end of it.

He'd already seen the end of it from the other side, in the kid's head. He saw it from this side now, and it was worse. A snap, deep inside her, her spine going. A scream that turned into a gurgle. Blood, everywhere, a fountain of it, coming out of her mouth. The boy's teeth. The doctor gone, out hunting, and the blonde woman losing control at the smell and being dragged out of the room by the red-wolf boy. The bronze-haired one with nothing but his own teeth to do it with, bending over her open belly. The baby lifted out. *Renesmee*, the girl whispered, through blood. *Let me hold her.* The baby's teeth going into her chest.

Then the heart stopping.

Then a syringe the length of his forearm going straight into her sternum, the boy driving it home with his own hand, emptying his venom into her heart. Then the boy biting her everywhere, wrists, throat, ankles, every big vein. Then fire. Three days of fire. Her screaming the whole time inside a body that couldn't move.

Harry had seen a fair bit in his life. He'd been to war at seventeen. He'd seen people die in every way the human body had of doing it. He'd still never seen anything quite like what this girl had decided to do to herself because she loved a dead man and wanted to keep his baby.

He didn't stop to have an opinion about it. He'd have one later. He'd have several.

He found what he'd come for. The *what*.

It came in pieces, in the blond doctor's voice mostly, a dozen conversations she'd half-heard through a fog of pain and sharp-eyed listened to after. Vampires didn't change. The venom froze them as they were. But the men's bodies had stayed what they'd been at the moment they died, every cell, and that included what they needed to make a child. The women's bodies froze too, and you couldn't grow a child in a womb made of stone. But a human woman's body still changed. Still could. And if a vampire and a human woman—

There was more. Chromosomes, the doctor had said, twenty-four pairs. Venom in the blood. A placenta like armour, hard as a vampire's skin, that no needle could get through. He didn't need all of it. He had enough. The kid was half human, half vampire, a hybrid born live out of a living mother who'd been carrying her for about a month before it nearly killed her. As far as anyone in this woman's head knew, she was the only one of her kind that had ever been.

Fine.

Filed.

He was about to come out when he found the last thing.

It was close to the surface, because she'd been thinking about it this morning. Her first hours as a vampire, sharp-edged and glittering. Her daughter in someone else's arms. The red-wolf boy sitting beside the baby on the floor of the big glass house. And a smell on her daughter. *His* smell, the boy's, all over the baby's skin.

And her finding out.

*You imprinted on my daughter?*

Harry stopped dead.

He went through it slow, then. Every word. The girl, three days a vampire, flying at the boy across the front lawn. The boy not even fighting back. Another wolf, a smaller one, sandy, *Seth*, getting between them and taking the hit for it, his shoulder smashing, his collarbone going with a sound like a gunshot. The girl screaming at the boy: *Nessie? You nicknamed my daughter after the Loch Ness Monster?*

Harry, despite himself, for half a second, liked her.

And then the explaining. The boy explaining. Older memories under it, the girl's human memories, fogged, but there. A beach, a bonfire. The boy telling her what the wolves did. What happened to them sometimes when they looked at someone. *Imprinting*. They didn't choose it. It happened on sight, the first time. One look and it was done, and it was for life. The wolf was tied to that person forever. Whatever she needed him to be, he'd be it. Brother, friend, protector. *Like gravity moves*, the boy had said. *Suddenly she's the only thing holding you to the planet.*

Sam and Emily. A man called Sam who'd imprinted on his girlfriend's cousin and left his girlfriend standing. Quil and Claire, a boy of sixteen and a girl of two. And now the red-wolf boy and a baby who'd been born that same morning.

And the girlfriend Sam had left standing.

The bitter one. The only girl who'd ever turned. The grey one, small, fast, mean as a snake. The one who couldn't stand being in Sam's pack, hearing Sam love someone else every second of every day, so she'd gone over to Jacob's. The one with a dead dad.

*Leah*.

Leah Clearwater.

He came out of Bella's head like a man coming up from the bottom of a swimming pool.

He left without bothering about a clean exit. Whatever he'd broken going in stayed broken, and he didn't look back to check.

He was standing in the snow. His heart was going a bit harder than he'd like. Smoke was still climbing off the far side of the field and the cold air tasted of burnt incense and the kid was still crying and Bella was on her knees in the slush with her hands over her face and her husband crouched in front of her snarling at Harry, and over on the left, the wolves had lost the plot entirely. He could hear them. Snarling, snapping, a scuffle of big bodies, the whining yelp of the grey one going up again over the top of it.

He turned his head and found her.

Small. Grey. Faster-looking than the others. Standing at the edge of the red wolf's pack with her claws buried in the frozen ground and every muscle in her body leaning toward him like a dog straining at a lead. Staring at him. She hadn't taken her eyes off him since the second he'd looked at her.

The second he'd looked at her.

That twinge, under his breastbone, right when he'd turned and looked at the wolves. He'd put it down to the Apparition. Long hop, Geneva to Washington, enough to give anyone a stitch.

It hadn't been the Apparition.

"Fuck me sideways," said Harry Potter, out loud, to the whole field.

Nobody answered him.

He stood there a second. Then, because he'd never in his life been able to leave a thing alone once he'd seen it, he looked.

At himself, this time. At his end.

He closed his eyes, and went in, the way he went into other people, only backwards. Into his own skull. Into the quiet, ordered, locked-down place he'd built there over a lot of years and a lot of practice, so that nobody would ever walk into his head uninvited again.

The line was right where the twinge had been.

He'd never seen anything like it. It came in from outside, from across the snow, from her, and it went into his chest right through the breastbone, bright and taut and humming like a wire in the wind. That was what the wolves' thing did, then. Tied a line between two people. Fair enough. He'd seen bonds before. Magical ones, a few, Unbreakable Vows, life debts. Wizards had a lot of ways of tying people together and most of them ended badly. He'd expected it to stop there, at the heart, where a thing like that ought to stop.

It kept going.

Through the heart and past it. Down. Further down than anything had any right to go, past the place in the middle of him where his magic sat, past the walls he'd put up, past the floor of the place where he kept himself, down into the dark underneath where he didn't go very often and didn't talk about at all.

And down there, at the bottom of the line, something looked back at him.

It was big. It was black, midnight-black, with a charcoal undercoat that showed when it moved, and it lay in the dark with its head up and its ears forward, the line running straight into its chest. It had green eyes. His eyes. It was looking up at him along the line with an expression he knew, because he saw it in the mirror on the days he wasn't trying very hard: interested, and calm, and not remotely sorry.

It was him. It had always been him. It was the part of him that didn't bother with words and didn't bother with rules and didn't bother with *Harry, maybe you shouldn't*. The part that ran. The part that, if he let it off the lead, could put its teeth through a car door. It had taken the line into itself like it had been waiting for one.

Its tail moved once, slow, against the dark.

Harry opened his eyes.

The grey wolf was still staring at him from across the snow. Still straining. Still making that awful thin whining noise under her breath.

He looked at her for a long moment.

"Love," he said, quietly, and he knew she'd hear it, because the wolf at the bottom of him told him she would. "You've got no idea what you've tied yourself to, Lassie. None."

The whining stopped.

He took a breath. A long one, all the way down, the cold air full of smoke and ash and venom and wet dog. Held it. Let it go.

Then he lifted his right hand, two fingers out, and there was a cigarette between them. It was simply there, without his reaching for it. He looked at the end of it and it caught, a small orange coal glowing in the grey light, and a thread of smoke curled up off it and mixed with the purple haze still drifting across the field.

He put it in his mouth and pulled on it, long, and let the smoke out through his nose.

Then he looked round at all of them: the vampires frozen on the snow, the mother on her knees, the father snarling, the kid crying on the back of the wolf who'd fallen for her the day she was born, the grey wolf who'd just fallen for him.

"Fuck me," he said. "You lot have got a fucked-up way of making babies."

### VIII. Jacob

Nessie was bleeding on him.

He could feel it. One warm drop, then another, soaking down through the fur at the back of his neck where she'd pressed her face. He could smell it, bright and sweet and Nessie, and he could feel her small hands knotted in his ruff, shaking, and the hiccupping little sobs going through her body into his.

She'd touched him. The second the stranger let go of her, she'd shoved both hands flat against his neck, and she'd shown him.

He'd never felt anything like it. A cold thin edge sliding into her, into *Nessie*, through the soft shining bubble of Bella's shield like a knife going through wet paper, and then through her, through everything she was, flipping her open, turning her pages. It had no face and no voice. Cold, and sharp, and fast, and *in*, and Nessie so small and so frightened, throwing everything she had at it and watching it walk round her pictures like they weren't even there.

Jacob had felt every second of it. He was still feeling it. Nessie couldn't stop showing it to him.

*I'll kill him.* He didn't know if he was thinking it at the pack or just thinking it. *I'll kill him, I'll rip his throat out, I'll—*

*You'll burn,* Embry said. *Jake. You saw. You'll burn and she'll be on your back when you do.*

That stopped him. It was the only thing that could have.

The pack mind was chaos. Had been since the stranger went into Nessie's head, and really, if Jacob was honest, since about ten seconds after he showed up. Seth's head was full of Leah, Leah, Leah and a strange miserable hope he kept trying to hide and couldn't. Quil kept looking from Leah to the stranger to Nessie and back, his thoughts tumbling over each other. Embry was the only one holding it together and even Embry was thinking about the trees.

And Leah.

Leah was standing in front of him.

Right in front of him. Between Jacob and the stranger, broadside on, teeth bared at her own Alpha, her ears flat to her skull and her hackles up in a ridge from her neck to her tail. She'd been there for a full minute. She'd got there the second he moved.

He played it back without wanting to. He couldn't help it; in here, you couldn't help anything. The stranger's eyes going to Nessie. Nessie going rigid on his back. Bella's gasp. And Jacob's whole body bunching to go, to cross the snow and put his teeth through the bastard, Nessie on his back or not.

And Leah, who'd been leaning toward the stranger for two solid minutes like a plant toward the sun, spinning round and slamming into his shoulder so hard she knocked him half a step sideways.

*NO.*

He'd never heard her think that loud. Not when Sam left. Not when her dad died.

*Get out of my way, Leah.*

*No. NO. You go for him, I go for you. I'll do it, Jacob. I swear to God I'll do it.*

*He's in her HEAD—*

*I KNOW. I know, I can feel you, I can feel all of it, I'm sorry, I'm sorry, I can't, I CAN'T—*

Seth had shoved himself between them at that, sandy and skinny and terrified, the way he'd shoved himself between Bella and Jacob that first morning and got his shoulder smashed for it. Quil had crowded in on one side, Embry on the other, and for a few seconds the whole of Jacob's pack had been a knot of snarling bodies in the snow while the stranger stood forty yards away going through Nessie's life like a filing cabinet.

Jacob had felt the Alpha voice rising in his chest. He'd felt the words coming. *Leah. Move.* One command, with the weight of the bloodline behind it, and she'd have to. She'd have no choice. She'd step aside whether she wanted to or not, whether every cell in her body was screaming at her to protect the man or not.

And he'd stopped.

He'd stopped with the words in his mouth, because he'd suddenly seen it from her side. Felt it from her side. Somebody standing over him with that voice, ordering him to step aside while someone hurt Nessie. Ordering him to stand there and *watch*.

He couldn't. He couldn't do that to her. Not to anyone. Not now that he knew.

So he'd held. All of them had held, in a tangle, in the snow, with Leah shaking in front of him and her eyes rolling back to the stranger every half-second, and Nessie crying on his back, and the whole thing coming apart at the seams.

Then the stranger had come out of Nessie and gone into Bella, and Bella had gone down.

Jacob had watched Bella's knees hit the snow and felt his heart go with them. Bella. Bella who'd survived everything, the tracker, the newborns, the pregnancy, the *birth*, Bella with the shield that had held against the Volturi's whole guard all morning, crumpling like somebody had cut her strings.

And now the stranger was standing there with a cigarette.

He'd said it out loud. To all of them. *Fuck me sideways.* And then the thing about Leah. Jacob had heard every word. Every wolf on the field had.

*You've got no idea what you've tied yourself to, Lassie.*

Leah had stopped whining.

She'd stopped like somebody had flipped a switch, and in the pack mind her thoughts, which had been a solid wall of screaming for two minutes, had gone abruptly, completely quiet. Then one thought came out of the quiet, very small, very flat.

*He knows.*

Then, a second later, louder:

*Did he just call me Lassie?*

*He did,* Seth said. He sounded dazed. *He called you Lassie.*

*Like the dog,* Quil said.

*I KNOW it's like the dog, Ateara.*

And then the cigarette, and the line about babies, and Jacob felt something hot and ugly come up in his throat that was half rage and half something else, something that felt horribly like shame.

*Fucked-up way of making babies.*

On the far left, Sam's pack had stopped pretending they weren't watching. They'd come across. Not far, a dozen yards maybe, but they'd broken their line and come toward Jacob's, Sam at the front, huge and black, Paul's silver shape at his shoulder with his head low and his lips peeled back. Jared. Collin and Brady hanging at the back like the kids they were. They couldn't hear any of this. They'd just watched Jacob's pack tear itself apart in the snow and Leah Clearwater, who'd *left* Sam's pack, bare her teeth at her own Alpha to protect a man who'd just burned the Volturi like paper.

Paul was growling at Leah. A low steady rumble. Jacob knew that growl. It meant *What the hell are you doing?*

Sam was silent. He was just looking at her.

Jacob felt Leah feel it. Felt her whole body go tight, and her lip come up, and a snarl come out of her at Sam that had two years in it.

*Don't,* she thought. Jacob didn't think she even knew she was thinking it at the pack. *Don't look at me like you understand. Don't you dare understand. You did this to me. You did this to me first, and now you get to look at me like you KNOW—*

*Lee,* Seth said, gently.

*Shut up. Shut up, Seth. Just—*

She didn't finish. The stranger had turned.

He was looking at Jacob now. Straight at him, across the snow, past Leah's shoulder, with the cigarette between two fingers and a thin line of smoke going up off the end of it. He looked at Jacob the way Jacob had seen Charlie look at a drunk he'd pulled over on the 101 at two in the morning: tired, and not surprised, and very much not amused.

His eyes went to Nessie on Jacob's back. Then back to Jacob.

"And you," the stranger said. "Cujo."

Jacob's lips came off his teeth.

"Yeah, growl all you like, mate." He tapped ash off the cigarette into the snow. "I've seen it. Her head and her mum's. I've seen you come round that doorway with murder on your face and I've seen what your face did when you saw her. She was *hours* old." He let the smoke out slow through his nose. "We are going to have words, you and me. Long ones. You'll do it with your mouth, in English, with a pair of trousers on, and you'll explain to me in very small words how a lad your age ends up tied for life to a newborn, and why everyone round here seems to think that's a lovely bit of folklore. Because I've got thoughts."

*You don't understand*, Jacob thought, furious, desperate, the words he'd said to Bella, to Charlie, to everyone who ever looked at him sideways. *She's my kid. She's my family. I'd never—I'm whatever she needs, I'm her brother, I'm her—*

Nobody outside the pack heard that. The stranger certainly didn't. But Jacob felt it go out into the pack mind and he felt the others hear it, and for the first time in four months, he felt Leah, who knew better than anyone what an imprint was and what it wasn't, *not* say anything snide about it.

She just felt sorry for him. She didn't hide it. That was worse.

The stranger was still looking at him. Then at Leah, standing broadside between them with her teeth still bared at her own Alpha.

Something moved at the corner of his mouth.

"Yeah," he said, mostly to himself. "That's going to be fun."

He turned away. Took another long drag. Blew it out at the sky.

"Right," he said, to everyone. "Paperwork."

### IX. Carlisle

He had heard that sound once before.

The crack. The sound the young man had made arriving, like a pistol going off in a small room. Carlisle had heard it in London in the winter of 1661, in the cellar of a house on Fish Street Hill, where his father had locked a widow called Margaret Hale on the strength of a neighbour's word and a dead cow. Carlisle had been twenty and had carried the lamp. They'd come down the next morning with the magistrate to take her out to the hanging, and the door had still been locked, and the chains had still been on the wall, and Margaret Hale had been gone. Carlisle had been standing in the street outside at dawn when it happened. He'd heard it through the cellar grate: a single sharp crack, like a pistol shot, and then nothing.

His father had called it devilry and hanged a different woman the following week, and Carlisle had carried the memory with him for three hundred and forty-odd years without ever finding anyone who could explain it.

He was fairly sure now that someone could.

The young man was standing in the snow with a cigarette in one hand, looking at all of them. He'd said *paperwork*. Carlisle had no idea what that meant here, today, in this field, and he was very much afraid to find out.

Behind him, he could hear his family. Esme's hand had found his back and stayed there. Rosalie was murmuring to Renesmee from where she'd crouched beside Jacob, low, soothing, *I know, baby, I know, it's over now*. Emmett was standing over Bella like a wall. Edward had his arms round his wife and his face in her hair, and Bella was shaking, and Carlisle had never in all their time together seen her shake.

He made himself step forward again.

"You said paperwork," he said.

"Yeah." The young man looked at him. He took a last pull on the cigarette and then it was simply gone, gone from his fingers between one moment and the next, and he let the smoke out. "Hang on, Doc. Got to make a call."

He held out his right hand, palm up, and something came out of it.

Light. Silver light, pouring up out of his palm like water out of a spring, bright enough that Carlisle's eyes narrowed against it. It gathered itself in the air over the snow, thickened, took a shape, and Carlisle heard the Irish behind him draw a single collective breath.

A stag.

It stood in the snow in front of him, enormous, made entirely of shining silver mist. Every hair of its coat was light. Its antlers branched up into the grey air like the crown of a tree in winter. It turned its great head and looked at Carlisle with eyes that were only brighter light, and he felt something come off it that he hadn't felt in a very long time, a clean cold steadiness like the air on a hilltop at dawn.

Its hooves didn't mark the snow.

The young man looked at it the way you'd look at an old dog.

"Right," he said to it. "Potter for the Confederation desk, Geneva. Situation in Forks is sorted. Ish." He scratched the back of his neck. "Volturi refused the dispersal order and moved on me. Leadership and guard are ash, the lot of them, the wives included. Also a Romanian named Vladimir, same reason, came at my back. Their witnesses scattered, forty-odd, all directions, so you'll want somebody watching the airports for a few days, they'll be twitchy and hungry." He paused. "No Muggle exposure. Nearest town's fourteen miles off and nobody here's daft enough to go near it."

The stag stood and listened.

"Second thing." He glanced across the snow, past Carlisle, to where Renesmee sat crying on Jacob's back with Rosalie's hand on her leg. His face didn't change. "There is an unregistered hybrid here. Half human, half vampire. Born live to a human mother about four months back. Mother didn't survive it as a human. She's one of them now. Kid looks four, grows fast, drinks blood, has a heartbeat, has a touch-based mind gift. Parents both present. She's not on any register we've got and I'd put my pension on her not being on anybody's. Far as anyone here knows she's the only one." He took a breath. "Requesting a registration team. A Healer who doesn't faint easily. And somebody from Beings who knows shapeshifters, because there's two packs of them here and they've got—" His eyes went, for less than a second, to the small grey wolf standing between Jacob and the rest of the world. "—quirks. I'll put it in the written report. Potter out."

The stag bowed its head once. Then it turned and leapt and was gone, a streak of silver light bounding away across the snow and into the trees, and the clearing was grey again.

For a moment nobody said anything.

"Registered," said Carlisle. It came out quietly.

"Yeah."

"She's a child."

"She is." The young man looked at him. "That's sort of the point, Doc. Unregistered, she's whatever the next lot in cloaks decides she is. You saw what this lot decided." He jerked his chin at the ash heaps on the far side of the field without looking at them. "Registered, she's a recognised magical being under Confederation protection. Means anyone who comes for her has to come through us. Means I've got a reason to give a toss what happens to her."

"And if we refuse?"

The young man considered that.

"You won't," he said. "You're not stupid. I've been in your daughter-in-law's head, I know you're not stupid. But I'm being polite, so I'm letting you ask."

Behind Carlisle, Edward made a noise. He'd lifted his head from Bella's hair and his eyes were black.

"You had no right," Edward said. His voice was shaking. "To go into her. Into either of them. No right."

The young man looked at him for a long moment.

"Probably not," he said. "Did it anyway." He nodded toward Bella. "For what it's worth, that shield of hers is good. Best I've ever come up against outside a lab. She held off the Volturi all morning with it and it cost me something to get through." His mouth twisted. "I'm sorry about the hole. It'll knit. It'll scar, a bit. They do."

Bella lifted her head.

Her face was white and her eyes had gone very dark, and she looked at him from the circle of Edward's arms with an expression Carlisle had never seen on her before.

"If you ever," she said, "go into my daughter's head again—"

"I won't need to." He met her look without blinking. "Got what I needed. And next time anyone wants to know what she is, they'll ask the register, which is the point of a register." He paused. "The rest of you." He raised his voice, just slightly, and it went out across the whole field. "Order stands. Everybody not named Cullen, disperse. Go home. Thanks for coming. Lovely day for it."

The Egyptians were gone before he finished the sentence. Amun had Kebi by the arm and they were in the trees, and Benjamin and Tia went after them with a single long look back. Peter and Charlotte went next. Mary and Randall. Stefan stood alone where Vladimir's ash had fallen for a long moment more, staring at it, and then he turned without a word and walked into the forest and did not look back.

The Irish held. The Amazons held. The Denalis held, Tanya still on her knees in the slush with Carmen's arms around her and Eleazar standing over them both, Kate beside them with Garrett's hand still on her arm.

Garrett met the young man's eye across the snow and lifted one shoulder in a shrug. "I'm with her," he said, nodding at Kate. "She's with them. I'd sooner not burn, but I'd sooner not leave either."

The young man looked at him a second. Then something moved at the corner of his mouth.

"Fair enough," he said. "Stay out of my way, Yankee Doodle."

Garrett's grin flashed, brief and startled.

Carlisle let his breath out slowly.

"The *rest* of you lot," the young man said, turning to look at the Cullens, "stay where I can see you till the team gets here. Shouldn't be long. Geneva's quick when they're properly frightened." He looked over at the wolves. "And you lot—"

He stopped.

The grey wolf was coming.

She'd stepped out from in front of Jacob. Carlisle hadn't seen her decide to do it. One moment she was standing broadside between her Alpha and the stranger with her teeth bared, and the next she was walking out across the snow, alone, slowly, her head low and her ears forward and her hackles still half-raised along her spine. Behind her, Jacob's pack had gone rigid. Sam's pack, a dozen yards off, had gone rigid too. Nobody moved to stop her. Carlisle saw the great black wolf at the front of Sam's pack take half a step after her and then stop himself, and stand, and lower his head.

She walked across forty yards of snow and ash, past the place where Vladimir had burned, and stopped about ten feet from the young man.

She was big. All the wolves were big. She was the smallest of them and her shoulder still came level with his chest. She stood there in the snow with her grey coat ruffling in the wind and her claws dug in and she stared at him, and Carlisle, who had seen Sam look at Emily and Jacob look at Renesmee, knew that stare.

He'd just never seen it with that much fury in it.

The young man looked back at her. He didn't move toward her. He didn't move away. He stood there with his hands in the pockets of his leather jacket and the snow going to slush round his boots, and for a long moment the only sounds in the clearing were Renesmee's hiccupping breaths and the wind in the pines.

Carlisle breathed in, without meaning to. Pine. Earth. Cold stone.

And under it, now that he was this close, now that he knew to look, the same thing he could smell coming off the grey wolf ten feet away. Musk. Fur. Frost. Something with teeth.

The young man tilted his head.

"Hello, Lassie," he said.

The grey wolf's lips peeled back off her teeth, all the way, a long silent snarl that showed every one of them.

One corner of his mouth went up.

"Yeah," said Harry Potter. "Thought you might say that."
